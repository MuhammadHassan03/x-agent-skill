The twelve

One command each.

Every skill ends the same way: your text in one copy-ready block, then "Reply yes to log it". A yes appends one line to a file on your disk. It never posts.

  • /x-postOne idea into a post: three hooks from 19 formulas, linted, one humanized draft.

    Tools: postscore.py, xcount.py. A link in the root post is a hard stop on a free account (score capped, draft blocked): it goes in a FIRST REPLY block, labelled observed, not coded. Anything missing a number becomes {{your number}} and the scorer blocks it.

  • /x-threadA long draft into a thread of 280-weighted-character posts, links moved to a FIRST REPLY block.

    Tool: thread.py. Splits at paragraph, then sentence, then word. Warns when a removed link leaves a dangling sentence.

  • /x-replyReplies under bigger accounts, checked against an approximation of the 0 to 3 reply rubric.

    Tool: replylint.py. Refuses compliment-only openers and stacked self-replies. The real rubric prompt is not published; the bands are an approximation.

  • /x-articleLong-form outline, draft, first-screen hook and an originality checklist.

    Premium feature, documented but not tested by the author. Free accounts get pointed to /x-thread.

  • /x-dmOne first message and two follow-ups for one specific person. Then stop.

    One at a time, written for that person, sent by hand. No batches, no merge fields. Drafting only.

  • /x-humanStrips invisible Unicode, dashes and stock phrases, then scores five checks.

    Tools: humanize.py, detect.py. Local heuristics, not a third-party detector. It never says a text is undetectable.

  • /x-profileBio, pinned post, banner and link, scored on a 100-point rubric.

    Data: rubric.json, eleven items. The skill never opens or edits your profile.

  • /x-viralRanks posts you noted down by hand by multiple over each account's own median.

    Tool: swipe.py. Never opens X, never drives a browser. You paste, it ranks.

  • /x-auditReads your own analytics CSV and ranks your posts by outlier multiple.

    Tool: xanalytics.py. Headers matched flexibly because X does not document the export. Needs 3 or more posts.

  • /x-algoRegenerates the pinned ranking-weights reference from a param.rs you paste.

    Tool: weights.py. Refuses to run without a commit hash. It never fetches anything.

  • /x-planYour week: what to post, which format, when, and which accounts to reply to.

    Reply-first plan for a small free account. Times are marked as guesses until your own data says otherwise.

  • /x-moneyOriginal Content Rewards eligibility tracker and ineligible-content linter.

    Tool: ocr.py. X publishes no per-impression rate, so it refuses to estimate earnings unless you supply your own. Premium program, not tested by the author.

What it will not do

  • × Post, like, follow, reply or DM
  • × Drive a browser or run in the page
  • × Scrape X or call its API
  • × Upload your text anywhere
  • × Invent a number you did not give
  • × Call anything undetectable

X's Automation Rules list scripting the website under "Don't" and say it may lead to permanent suspension. So nothing here acts on X. Read the page.

How it works

Idea in, your own edit out.

The human stays in the loop for every step that touches X.

  1. 01

    Bring one real thing

    Something that happened, with a number you actually have. No number, no line: it becomes a placeholder.

  2. 02

    Draft and check

    Three hooks, one draft, counted the way X counts, then run through the checks that fit.

  3. 03

    Edit it by hand

    Change a word, cut a line, add a detail only you know. The skill shows the final block.

  4. 04

    You post it

    On x.com, yourself. Reply "yes" and one line is appended to a log on your disk. That is all "yes" does.

The tools

Ten tools that actually run.

Python 3.9+, standard library only, no network, UTF-8, CRLF tolerant, exit non-zero on bad input. Pick one to see its real output.

Counts a post the way X does: weighted characters, CJK and most emoji weigh 2, every URL weighs 23. Vendored from the public twitter-text config.

  remaining 204, urls 1 (each 23), weight-2 characters 0

What I actually measured

The scorer does not predict likes.

Tested within each account on 3 of 41 shards of a public tweet corpus: 6.4M tweet rows from 2019-01-01 to 2022-12-31, 1,500 accounts, 270,880 tweets scored. A hit is 3x or more that account's median likes (37,861); a miss is under 1x (131,549). Aggregates only; no tweet text is in the repo.

Within-account AUC, hits vs misses, over 1,402 accounts. 0.50 is a coin flip.

  • Hook score0.500 (0.494 to 0.506)
  • Full postscore, link cap off0.475 (0.469 to 0.482)
  • Full postscore, link cap on (default)0.449 (0.442 to 0.456)
  • Baseline: tweet length alone0.592 (0.584 to 0.600)
  • Baseline: number in first line0.527 (0.521 to 0.532)

The tick marks 0.50. The score does not forecast likes, and a trivial length feature beats it. It was not tuned on this data afterwards. So postscore.py is a lint for the problems it names (placeholders, bait, thread openers, links in the body, over length), not a prediction. The link hard stop lowers the full-score AUC from 0.475 (cap off, --allow-link) to 0.449 (cap on): in this corpus posts with links got more likes, so capping them points the wrong way here. The corpus predates the ranker and the link suppression, so the link cap is a policy choice, not a finding.

Mean difference in log2 of the multiple, within account. Positive means more likes than that account's norm.

Read carefully: in 2019 to 2022 data, posts with links and hashtag walls got more likes than the same account's other posts, the opposite of what the scorer penalises. The corpus ends in April 2023, before Premium reach changes, the open-sourced ranking code and Original Content Rewards, and likes are not reach. So the link advice rests on a 2025 outside observation (Buffer, 18.8M posts), labelled "observed, not coded", not on anything measured here.

Spearman correlation with follower count: raw likes 0.566, outlier multiple 0.025. Raw likes mostly measure account size; the multiple removes it.

The fine print

What it will not claim.

It cannot know how a post will perform

The measurement above says so. The score is a lint, not a forecast.

Nothing is undetectable

/x-human runs five local heuristics. They are not GPTZero, Originality, Copyleaks, Winston or Turnitin, and call none of them. Posts "created or posted using automated means" are ineligible for Original Content Rewards. Read and edit every post.

The reply rubric is not published

The open-sourced code scores replies 0 to 3 with a Grok model when the parent or root has over 250,000 followers, but the prompt is excluded "to reduce gameability". replylint.py is an approximation. Smaller accounts go through a different spam classifier.

No coded link penalty was found

Link advice is "observed, not coded": a measurement from outside, not a rule in the ranking parameters.

Weights scale predicted probabilities, not counts

The file's own comments call "one report cancels 468 likes" wrong. The weights here are the ones in the file at the pinned commit (like 0.66, click 0.3, dwell 0.05, not-interested -52.272), not the older figures many guides quote.

No earnings estimates

X publishes no per-impression rate. /x-money refuses to estimate unless you supply your own rate, and labels the result as your assumption.

Premium features are untested

Articles, 25,000-character posts, Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions are documented from X's pages, last checked 2026-10-21. The Subscriptions gate is unverified; check the app.

Numbers go stale

Every one is listed in references/stale.md with its date and source. The weights table is regenerated by /x-algo and pinned to a commit.

What was tested, and where

Locally on Python 3.15 and 3.9.25 on Windows, and in CI on 3.9, 3.11 and 3.13 on ubuntu and windows. Not tested on macOS.

Install

Three ways in.

You need Python 3.9 or newer for the tools; nothing to pip install. After installing, copy templates/voice.md to ~/.claude/x/voice.md and fill it in, or give Claude three of your own posts and ask it to write your voice file.

In Claude Code:

/plugin marketplace add MuhammadHassan03/x-agent-skill
/plugin install x-agent@x-agent-skill

Plugin skills are namespaced, so the commands are /x-agent:x-post, /x-agent:x-thread and so on.

Keep the x-* skill folders side by side: several import from x-post. The references/ and templates/ folders stay in the clone.

macOS and Linux:

git clone https://github.com/MuhammadHassan03/x-agent-skill.git
mkdir -p ~/.claude/skills
cp -r x-agent-skill/skills/x-* ~/.claude/skills/

Windows, PowerShell (cp -r does not work there):

git clone https://github.com/MuhammadHassan03/x-agent-skill.git
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force "$HOME\.claude\skills" | Out-Null
Copy-Item -Recurse x-agent-skill\skills\x-* "$HOME\.claude\skills\"

No Claude Code at all: paste one SKILL.md at the top of a chat and it runs as a mode. You lose the Python tools, which is most of the point of /x-post, /x-reply and /x-human, but the writing rules still apply.

https://github.com/MuhammadHassan03/x-agent-skill/tree/master/skills

Skills say python3. On Windows use python (or py): python3 there is usually the Microsoft Store shortcut and fails with "Python was not found".

30-day dogfood

In progress. Numbers land 2026-11-19.

Day 1 of 30 starts on 2026-10-21.

One free account, 2026-10-21 to 2026-11-19: one post a day, 5 to 10 replies, link in the first reply, a real hand edit on every draft. On day 30 the author exports their own analytics CSV and runs measurement/dogfood/report.py, which prints aggregates only. The result goes in the README unedited, good or bad.

One account over one month shows nothing about cause. Until then there are no dogfood numbers, and none are implied anywhere on this page. Read the routine.
